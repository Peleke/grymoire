Óss derives from Elder Futhark's Ansuz (god/Odin) but in Younger Futhark also means "estuary" or "river mouth"—where waters meet and mingle.

## Meaning

The divine, particularly Odin, and the place where different waters converge. Both meanings connect to speech and breath—Odin's gifts to humanity.

## In the Viking Age

The double meaning reflects the Viking worldview: gods and nature intertwined, the sacred found in landscape.
