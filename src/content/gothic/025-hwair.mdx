Ƕair means "cauldron" or "kettle" — the vessel of transformation.

The cauldron is where raw becomes cooked, ingredients become meal, ore becomes metal. It is the container of alchemy, the space where change happens under controlled conditions. Every kitchen is a laboratory; every pot is a crucible.

Wulfila invented this letter for a sound Greek lacked: the "hw" at the start of words like *ƕas* (who) and *ƕar* (where). English preserved this sound until recently — "which" was once pronounced "hwich," "where" was "hwere." We've mostly lost it now, but Gothic held it.

Numerically, Ƕair carries 700. Seven centuries. The legendary span, the duration of fairy tales and mythic ages. Seven hundred years is long enough for empires to rise and fall, for forests to grow where cities stood.

The cauldron connects these ideas: long transformation, slow change, the cooking that takes centuries. Nations are made in cauldrons. Cultures simmer for generations before they're done.

What is cooking in your life? What slow transformation is underway that won't be finished for years? Ƕair reminds you: some things cannot be rushed. Let the cauldron do its work.
