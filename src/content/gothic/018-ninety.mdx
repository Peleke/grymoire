This letter has no name because it has no sound.

It exists only as a numeral: 90. Nine tens. The highest double-digit before 100. Wulfila included it in his alphabet not for writing words but for writing numbers — a curious thing, a letter that cannot be spoken.

The symbol resembles Greek Koppa in its archaic form, borrowed specifically for numerical purposes. In Greek, Koppa had already fallen silent by the Classical period, retained only for counting. Wulfila preserved this ghost.

Ninety sits at a threshold. One more ten and you reach the first three-digit number. Ninety is anticipation, the inhale before the leap, the moment when the old count is almost exhausted and the new order waits to begin.

In numerology, 9 is completion and 10 is new beginning. 90 amplifies this: completion multiplied by completion, the end of endings before the true restart. It is the pause that precedes transformation.

There is wisdom in silence. Not every element of a system needs to speak. Some things exist to structure, to count, to mark position without claiming attention. The silent letter teaches: presence without voice is still presence.

What in your life does its work without asking for recognition?
