The final letter. The second silence.

Like its counterpart at 90, this letter has no sound. It exists only to write the number 900 — nine hundreds, the last station before one thousand. It is pure mathematics, position without pronunciation.

The symbol derives from Greek Sampi (Ͳ), an archaic letter already obsolete by Classical times. Wulfila salvaged it, as he salvaged Koppa for 90. The Gothic alphabet is partly a museum of Greek letters that Greek itself discarded.

Nine hundred is anticipation crystallized. One more hundred and you reach the first four-digit number. The whole counting system is about to shift, to expand into a new order of magnitude. This is the precipice.

In numerology, 900 combines the completion of 9 with the power of 100 — completion amplified, ending raised to its highest expression within the three-digit realm. After 900, after 999, everything changes. You enter the thousands.

Perhaps this is why the letter is silent. What could you say at the threshold of transformation? The moment before the leap, before the new era, before the change that changes the nature of change itself — that moment is wordless.

You have reached the end of the Gothic alphabet. But endings are beginnings in disguise.
