Iggws is named for Ingwaz — the Germanic god of fertility, ancestors, and the harvest.

Ingwaz was the divine ancestor of the Ingvaeones, the North Sea Germanic peoples. He represented the seed: human seed, grain seed, the continuity of life through generations. Where Teiws governed war and justice, Ingwaz governed growth and generation.

The Romans identified him with Freyr, the Norse god of fertility. The Angles who settled Britain may have brought his cult with them — "England" might ultimately derive from "Ingwaz-land." Gods leave traces in geography.

Numerically, Iggws carries 600. Six centuries. The span of a genealogy, the depth of an ancestral line. Six hundred years encompasses more than a dozen generations — enough to be genuine ancestry, enough for individuals to blur into lineage.

This is fitting. Ingwaz is the god of what continues, of what outlasts any single life. The seed planted by your great-great-grandparents may still grow in you. The crops planted in you may feed your great-great-grandchildren.

What are you planting that will outlast you? What seed are you carrying that was planted long before your birth? Iggws reminds: you are both ancestor and descendant, both planter and harvest.
