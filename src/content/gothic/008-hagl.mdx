Hagl means "hail" — frozen destruction falling from the sky.

For agricultural peoples, hail was catastrophe. A summer storm could destroy a season's work in minutes, turning grain to pulp, hope to despair. The Goths knew this terror and named a letter after it.

But hail is also transformation. Water becomes ice becomes water again. Destruction makes way for new growth. The field flattened by hail will grow back wilder, stronger, different.

Numerically, Hagl carries 8. In Christian numerology — the system Wulfila's Goths were adopting — 8 is the number of resurrection, of new beginnings. Christ rose on the eighth day (the day after the Sabbath, the first day of a new week). Baptismal fonts often have eight sides.

The pairing seems paradoxical: destruction and resurrection in the same letter. But perhaps the Goths understood what we forget: that some new beginnings require endings first. The hail that destroys the old crop makes room for the new.

What in your life needs a storm to clear it?
