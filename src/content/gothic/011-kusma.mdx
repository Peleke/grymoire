Kusma means "sprout" — the first green shoot breaking through soil.

After the ice of Eis, the emergence of Kusma. This is the sequence encoded in the alphabet itself: winter gives way to spring, stillness yields to growth. The Goths were close enough to the land to feel this rhythm in their bones.

A sprout is pure potential. It could become anything: oak or grain, flower or weed. At the moment of emergence, all futures are open. The seed has committed to growth but not yet to form.

Numerically, Kusma carries 20. Two completed decades. The threshold of the third. In the Gothic counting system, 20 marks a turning — the tens are no longer single digits, the numbers are building toward the hundreds.

The letter descends from Greek Kappa, a strong consonant, a sound of crisp beginnings. *K* cuts; it doesn't murmur. Even the phonetics suggest emergence, breaking through.

Consider the sprouts in your own life — the projects half-started, the skills barely begun, the relationships just forming. Each one is a Kusma moment: fragile, full of potential, requiring your protection and patience.

What are you growing?
