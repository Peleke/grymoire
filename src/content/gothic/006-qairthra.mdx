Qaírþra is the mystery letter.

Its exact meaning is uncertain — scholars have proposed various reconstructions, none definitive. What we know: it represented the "kw" sound, rare in Gothic, used mainly in loanwords and specific contexts.

The letter derives from Greek Koppa, an archaic character already falling out of use by the Classical period. Wulfila preserved what Greece was forgetting — a pattern that defines the Gothic alphabet itself.

Numerically, Qaírþra carries 6. One less than the sacred seven. Six is incompleteness, potential not yet realized, the day before the Sabbath. It is the number of work not yet finished.

Perhaps this is fitting for a letter whose meaning remains uncertain. Qaírþra reminds us that not everything can be recovered, that the past keeps some secrets. The Goths who wrote this letter knew what it meant; we can only approximate.

Some mysteries are invitations. The gap in our knowledge is a door, not a wall. What would you name this letter, if naming were yours to do?
