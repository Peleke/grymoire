Teiws is named for Tīwaz — the Germanic god of war, justice, and oaths.

Tīwaz was the sky father, possibly the oldest of the Germanic gods. He gave his name to Tuesday (Tīw's day). In the myths, he sacrificed his right hand to bind the wolf Fenrir, exchanging personal wholeness for cosmic order. The god of justice paid a price for justice.

By Wulfila's time, the Goths were becoming Christian. Yet they named a letter after the old god. This wasn't contradiction but continuity — the shape of the sacred changing, the structure of the sacred remaining. The new faith was grafted onto the old rootstock.

Numerically, Teiws carries 300. The number of Spartans at Thermopylae. The measure of legendary last stands, of holding the line when the line cannot be held. Three hundred is courage quantified, sacrifice made countable.

The pairing is apt. Tīwaz sacrificed his hand; the Spartans sacrificed everything. Both understood that some things matter more than survival. Justice. Duty. The oath once given.

What would you sacrifice your hand for? What line would you hold at any cost? Teiws asks you to know your answer before the question is asked.
