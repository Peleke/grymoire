Raida means "ride" or "journey" — movement with purpose.

The Goths were a traveling people. Before they settled in the Roman borderlands, they had migrated from Scandinavia across the Baltic, through the forests of what is now Poland, down to the Black Sea steppes. To be Gothic was to ride, to move, to never be entirely at rest.

The word connects to English "ride" and "road," German "reiten." This is primal Germanic vocabulary, embedded deep in the languages of peoples who measured life in journeys.

Numerically, Raida carries 100. The first three-digit number. The century. A full cycle of the decimal system. Hundred is completion at a new order of magnitude — not just finishing a count but transcending the counting system itself.

The pairing creates meaning: the journey (Raida) leads to completion (100). You don't reach the century by standing still. Movement is how you get to fullness; the ride is what carries you to the destination.

But perhaps the deeper truth is that the ride *is* the destination. The Goths who gave this letter its name didn't travel to arrive; they traveled because traveling was who they were.

Where is your journey taking you? Or are you traveling to travel?
