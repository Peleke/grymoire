Aíƕus means "horse" — the animal that made the Goths who they were.

The Gothic tribes were horse people. They swept across Europe on horseback, their mobility their greatest weapon. The horse meant speed, status, and striking power. To name a letter after the horse is to name it after freedom itself.

The word connects to Latin *equus* and survives in modern English "equestrian." Indo-European peoples carried their word for horse across continents, because they carried the horse itself.

Numerically, Aíƕus carries 5. The five fingers of the hand. The five senses. The human number, the number of agency and action. A horse without a rider is just an animal; with a rider, it becomes an extension of human will.

In the Gothic Bible, horses appear in prophecy and power — the apocalyptic riders, the war-mounts of kings. The letter 𐌴 would have evoked this thunder for any Goth who knew his letters.

Some animals we domesticate. Others domesticate us. The horse did both.
