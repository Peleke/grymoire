Paírþra is uncertain — one of the Gothic letters whose name we cannot reconstruct with confidence.

Scholars have proposed "lot" or "dice cup" — something connected to casting lots, to games of chance, to the mechanics of fate. If true, this places divination at the heart of the Gothic alphabet. The future was not known but could be consulted.

Casting lots appears throughout the Bible Wulfila translated: the soldiers casting lots for Christ's garment, the apostles casting lots to replace Judas. Chance and providence interweave; the random throw reveals the divine will. This is the paradox of lots: apparent randomness expressing deeper order.

Numerically, Paírþra carries 80. The patriarch's years — Moses's age when he confronted Pharaoh. Eighty is strength sustained into advanced age, vigor that defies expectation, power accumulated over decades.

Perhaps the connection is this: to cast lots is to accept that not everything is in your control. The old learn this lesson; the young resist it. By 80, you know that chance shapes life as much as choice. You throw the dice, but you don't control the fall.

What role does chance play in your story? What lots have fallen for you?
