Aza opens the Gothic alphabet as it opened the world: with breath.

The letter derives from Greek Alpha, which itself descends from Phoenician Aleph — the ox, the leader of the herd. By the time Wulfila adapted it for his Bible translation in the 4th century, the ox had become pure sound: the open vowel, the beginning of speech.

In Gothic numerology, Aza carries the value of 1. Unity. The indivisible. Before multiplication, before complexity, there is only this: a single mark, a single sound, a single step forward.

When you speak the "a" in *atta* (father) or *aíþs* (oath), you're echoing a shape that has traveled from Phoenician traders to Greek philosophers to Gothic bishops — and now to you.

Every alphabet is a technology of memory. Aza is where that technology boots up.
