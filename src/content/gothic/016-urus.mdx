Urus means "aurochs" — the wild ancestor of domestic cattle, now extinct.

The aurochs was massive, fierce, and dangerous. Standing six feet at the shoulder, with horns that could disembowel a hunter, it represented primal power that civilization could never fully contain. The Goths knew this animal; they hunted it, feared it, respected it.

To domesticate the aurochs was one of humanity's great achievements. From this wild beast came the docile cow, the patient ox, the source of milk and leather and labor. But something was lost in the taming — the voltage of the wild, the untameable essence.

Numerically, Urus carries 70. The traditional span of a complete human life ("threescore and ten"). Seventy is fullness, the years that encompass a whole person from birth to wise old age.

The pairing suggests something: wild strength belongs to the complete life. The person who lives fully doesn't domesticate all their wildness. Something of the aurochs remains — the part that cannot be contained, that doesn't fit neatly into social expectations.

What wildness have you preserved? What aurochs still runs in you?
