Dags means "day" — the fundamental unit of human time.

Before clocks, before calendars, there was the day: light rising, light falling, the rhythm that organizes all other rhythms. The Gothic word survives almost unchanged in modern English and German (*Tag*). Some words are too essential to evolve.

Numerically, Dags carries 4. Four corners. Four winds. Four seasons. The number that frames the world, that gives it edges and orientation. If 3 is the first stable shape, 4 is the first stable container.

The letter descends from Greek Delta, the triangle, but acquired Germanic meaning. In Gothic mouths, the abstract shape became a word for dawn and dusk, for work and rest, for the basic bargain we make with time.

*Dags* appears throughout the Gothic Bible, marking the rhythm of creation and salvation alike. "On the third *dag*..." The story of faith moves in days.
