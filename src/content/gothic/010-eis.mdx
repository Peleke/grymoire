Eis means "ice" — the element that stops time.

Ice preserves. Bodies, food, seeds, secrets. What falls into ice may emerge centuries later, unchanged. The Goths knew ice as winter's grip, as danger on rivers, as the force that could lock a world in stillness.

But ice also kills. The frozen lake that looks solid. The cold that takes fingers, then feet, then life. Ice is beautiful and lethal, patient and absolute.

Numerically, Eis carries 10. The return to one, but at a higher order. 1 + 0. The first compound number, the base of the decimal system. Ten is unity reconstituted, the cycle that begins again but carries its history forward.

The letter descends from Greek Iota, the smallest letter, the one Christ said would not pass from the law. *Not one iota.* Something about smallness and permanence, about the tiny marks that outlast empires.

Ice teaches patience. It does not hurry. It does not argue. It simply waits, and in waiting, transforms everything it touches.

What are you preserving? What might you need to let thaw?
