Manna means "human being" — *man* in the old, ungendered sense. Humanity itself.

This is the letter that names us. When a Gothic scribe wrote 𐌼, they were writing a word for what they were. The alphabet contains its user, the code includes the coder. This is either humility or hubris, depending on how you look at it.

The word survives everywhere: German *Mann*, English "man," Sanskrit *manu*. Across the Indo-European world, this sound meant us. The ones who name things. The ones who write alphabets.

Numerically, Manna carries 40. The number of trial. Forty days of rain, forty years in the desert, forty days of temptation. Forty is the crucible: the period of testing that transforms whoever survives it.

Perhaps this is fitting. To be human is to be tested. To be Manna is to be refined by difficulty, shaped by what we endure. The alphabet places us at the number of trial because that's what we are: beings defined by our capacity to suffer, adapt, and transcend.

You are Manna. You are the one who reads these letters and makes meaning from marks. What trial is shaping you right now?
