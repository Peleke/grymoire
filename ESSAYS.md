## Node 0: Rome

### BOOK A (History/Culture): "Alaric: A Song, an Heir, and the Ruler of All"

**Title unpacked:**
- "A Song" = *carmen* (from Ovid's *carmen et error*, his explanation for exile)
- "the Ruler of All" = Ala-rics (Gothic etymology)
- "an Heir" = Guðríður as heir to the Christianization of the Germanic peoples

**This is the FRAME story.** Alaric gets us Rome, the Goths, the fall of an empire. It sets the stage for the entire journey.

#### Structural Sketch

1. **Roman Moesia / Ovid's exile**
   - Augustus banishes Ovid to Tomis (modern Constanța, Romania) — *carmen et error*
   - Tomis sits on the Black Sea coast, edge of the Roman world
   - Tomis sacked ca. 270 AD by Goths — the people who will produce Alaric

2. **The Aurelian Walls**
   - Built 270–275 AD, roughly contemporaneous with the Gothic raids
   - Rome fortifies itself for the first time since the Servian Walls
   - Foreshadowing: walls built against exactly the threat Alaric will fulfill

3. **Alaric's life**
   - Birth (c. 370 AD), raised in Roman Moesia
   - Military service *for* Rome — fought against the Franks
   - The betrayal / political exclusion that turns him against Rome
   - The Sack of 410

4. **Stilicho**
   - Half-Vandal, half-Roman, Rome's best general
   - Defeated the Vandals and Suebi in 406
   - Executed in 408 for being too Germanic — destroyed the one man who could have stopped Alaric
   - The question: *who counted as Roman?*

5. **Flashback: Brennus and the Senones (c. 387 BC)**
   - The Gauls breach the Servian Walls — *dies Alliensis*
   - Cisalpine Gauls / Senones — Celtic presence in Italy predating the Germanic one
   - Structural echo: Rome sacked by "barbarians" from the north, 800 years apart

6. **Alaric's burial beneath the Busento**
   - The river diverted, the king buried in the riverbed, the enslaved workers killed
   - The grave never found

7. **Personal invocation** — the author's blood, the journey begins

### BOOK A (History/Culture): "Guðríður: The Heir"

**This is the HEART of Rome.** Alaric is spectacle; Guðríður is the person we follow. The pilot essay for Book A.

#### Structural Sketch

1. **Who was she?**
   - Guðríður Þorbjarnardóttir — born in Iceland, traveled to Vinland (North America), returned, pilgrimaged to Rome
   - The farthest-traveled woman of the medieval world
   - Mother of Snorri Þorfinnsson — first child of European descent born in the New World

2. **The pilgrimage**
   - She arrives in Rome years after Iceland's conversion at Öxarárfoss (c. 999/1000)
   - The politics-not-war conversion — contrast with Alaric's martial Christianity centuries earlier
   - What was it like to walk these streets as a woman? As a *Norse* woman in medieval Christendom?

3. **The women's history we don't have**
   - With whom did she keep company? What stories did the women she traveled with tell?
   - What jokes did the ladies tell about the men they saw?
   - What have we forgotten in treating history as, literally, *his* story?
   - The saga sources give us her movements; we have to imagine her inner life
   - This is not invention — it's the honest acknowledgment of what the record doesn't preserve

4. **The heir**
   - She inherits the Christianization thread Alaric carried in its Arian form
   - But she carries it differently — as faith chosen, not faith conquered
   - Her name survives; her voice doesn't. We write toward the silence.

5. **The inversion**
   - She went Iceland → Vinland → back → Rome → back to Iceland
   - We go Rome → north → Iceland → America
   - Her path, reversed and extended. The project's DNA.

6. **Personal invocation**
   - Why this journey? Why trace *her* path?
   - The author's own blood — Norwegian grandfather, the line from there to here
   - Ðirðug begins where she ended

### BOOK B (Historical Linguistics): "Gothic and Latin — The Languages of Rome's Undoing"

**The linguistic companion to Node 0.** This is Track B — rigorous but angled at the personal linguistic journey.

#### Structural Sketch

1. **The *atta unsar* outside the Aurelian Walls**
   - Opening image: reading the Gothic Lord's Prayer aloud near the walls Alaric breached
   - What Gothic sounded like — phonology, stress, the feel of it in the mouth

2. **Ulfilas and the Gothic Bible**
   - The oldest substantial text in any Germanic language
   - Ulfilas's alphabet — Greek base with runic and Latin additions
   - What survives: the Codex Argenteus in Uppsala (foreshadowing the Adam of Bremen thread)

3. **Alaric's bilingualism**
   - Gothic + Latin — a man between two linguistic worlds
   - Code-switching in late antiquity — who spoke what to whom?
   - The political weight of language choice

4. **Old Norse and Gothic — cousins separated by centuries**
   - Guðríður's language and Alaric's: East Germanic vs. North Germanic
   - Shared roots, divergent paths — what connects them, what doesn't
   - The reconstructed Proto-Germanic that both descend from

5. **The author's linguistic position**
   - Arriving in Rome speaking Tuscan Italian, carrying Norwegian
   - Reaching for the dead languages underneath
   - The learner's first step — what does it feel like to speak Gothic for the first time?
   - This is the beginning of Track B's personal thread: the vernacular journey

## Node 1: Fiesole → Florence

### BOOK A (History/Culture): "From Viesul to Firenze"

**The Etruscans, absorbed. The question of the foreign, inherited from Stilicho.**

#### Structural Sketch

1. **Fiesole before Rome**
   - Viesul, Visl, Vipsul — the settlement that predates Roman Tuscany
   - What was here before the Republic reached north?

2. **The question from Rome: who counted as Roman?**
   - Stilicho was expelled for being too Germanic. The Etruscans were absorbed for being too useful.
   - Their language vanished but their rituals remained — augury, haruspicy, the reading of lightning
   - Same city, same anxiety about the foreign, different centuries

3. **The twelve and divination**
   - Rome sent 12 boys to Fiesole to study augury
   - Twelve, the number Odin claims for his own names
   - Ovid's Metamorphoses touches this world — throwback to *carmen et error* from Node 0
   - The thread of sacred knowledge passing between cultures that officially don't acknowledge each other

4. **Forward pull to Milan**
   - North of here, in Cisalpine Gaul, the Senones whose Brennus sacked Rome held territory that became Mediolanum
   - The Celtic thread from Rome gets its own ground

### BOOK B (Historical Linguistics): "Turskum Nomen — The Language No Family Claims"

**The Etruscan naming cascade and the opening of the Tyrsenian thread.**

#### Structural Sketch

1. **The naming cascade**
   - Greeks: Tyrrhenoi → Mare Tyrrhenum, the Tyrrhenian Sea
   - Romans: Tusci, Etrusci — from "turskum nomen" (Umbrian attestation)
   - The *tursci base form → Etrusci through metathesis and epenthesis → Toscana
   - Autonym: Rasenna, or Rasna
   - Ionian exonym: Tyrsenoi

2. **Tyrsenian linguistics**
   - A proposed pre-Indo-European family: Etruscan, Rhaetian, Lemnian
   - What connects these isolates? What's the evidence? What's contested?
   - This thread continues through the Alpine passes — Rhaetian at Como (Node 3)

3. **The decipherment problem**
   - What we can read of Etruscan (the script is known; the language is not)
   - What we know vs. what we don't
   - The difference between decipherment and understanding

4. **The author's Tuscan**
   - Speaking modern Tuscan Italian on the hills where Etruscan was spoken
   - The layers visible in the landscape — da Vinci experimented near here too
   - What does it feel like to speak the descendant language in the ancestor's place?

## Node 2: Milan

### BOOK A (History/Culture): "Medhelanon — The Middle of the Sanctuary"

**Temporal reversal: from Stilicho (406) backward to the Insubres, then forward through the Germanic layers.**

#### Structural Sketch

1. **Stilicho's defense (406 AD)**
   - The half-Vandal general defending Roman Mediolanum
   - Thread from Rome — he'll be dead in two years, executed for his blood

2. **Roman Mediolanum**
   - Imperial capital of the western court
   - Administrative center, not sacred — the Romans repurposed the place

3. **Celtic Medhelanon**
   - The Insubres (Gaulish tribe of Cisalpine Gaul) called it Medhelanon: "the middle of the sanctuary"
   - The site was aligned, sacred — a Celtic ritual center before it was a Roman one
   - The augury/divination parallel from Fiesole: the Insubres read the land differently, but they read it

4. **The Germanic layers**
   - Ostrogoths take Milan → Lombards take it from them
   - Lombardy from the Langobards ("long-beards")
   - A city that's been Celtic, Roman, Ostrogothic, Lombard, and Italian

5. **Forward pull to Como**
   - Insubres' territory extended to the lakes — Como is their northern edge
   - The climb from the sacred plain to the Alpine passes

### BOOK B (Historical Linguistics): "Gallo-Italic, Lombardic, Tuscan — The Genealogy of Milanese"

**Three languages in one city: the Celtic substrate, the Germanic superstrate, and the Tuscan we'll speak in the videos.**

#### Structural Sketch

1. **Gallo-Italic**
   - Milanese is Gallo-Italic, not Tuscan — it carries the Celtic substrate in its phonology
   - What Cisalpine Gaul left behind in the sound of the city

2. **Lombardic**
   - Germanic superstrate from the Langobards
   - Traces in modern Italian: words, place-names, legal terminology
   - The difference between Lombardic influence and the later High German that came through the Hanseatic trade

3. **Modern Milanese vs. Tuscan**
   - The Italian we speak in the field recordings is Florentine-based standard Italian
   - Milanese is a different language — mutual intelligibility is limited
   - What happens when you try to speak Tuscan with Milanese speakers: the friction, the humor, the code-switching

4. **The rooftop**
   - Track B's Milan moment: a day-long conversation between me (Tuscan) and a Milanese speaker
   - The linguistic differences made audible, visible, felt
   - This is the "stumble in the vernacular" moment for Milan

## Node 3: Como

### BOOK A (History/Culture): "Pliny's Lake and the Road Over the Alps"

**Transition node — the plain ends, the passes begin.**

#### Structural Sketch

1. **Pliny the Elder**
   - Born at the foot of these mountains
   - The naturalist who died at Vesuvius — curiosity as a fatal trait
   - His nephew's letters survive; the lake bears the family name loosely

2. **The route everyone has taken**
   - The Cimbri, the Lombards, the medieval merchants, the modern tourists
   - The same Alpine passes for two thousand years
   - Geography as destiny — there are only so many ways through the mountains

3. **Forward pull**
   - From the Italian plain to the Germanic world on the other side
   - The Insubres' territory ended at the lakes; beyond here, the linguistic landscape shifts

### BOOK B (Historical Linguistics): "Rhaetian Inscriptions and the Tyrsenian Thread"

**The Tyrsenian substrate resurfaces in Alpine rock.**

#### Structural Sketch

1. **Rhaetian**
   - The proposed Tyrsenian family: Etruscan (Fiesole) → Rhaetian (Alps) → Lemnian (Aegean)
   - Rhaetian inscriptions in the Alpine region — what they look like, what we can read

2. **The Alpine passes as linguistic frontiers**
   - Below: Romance (Italian, Romansh)
   - Above: Germanic (Swiss German, Bavarian)
   - In the rock: a substrate older than both
   - The moment you cross and the sound of the world changes

3. **The author at the pass**
   - Leaving Italy, entering the Germanic sound-world
   - What changes in the ear, the mouth, the rhythm of speech

## Node 4: Zurich

### BOOK A (History/Culture): "Helvetica — From Province to Typeface"

**The exhale between the dense Italian arc and the Rhineland. Lighter in tone.**

#### Structural Sketch

1. **Helvetia**
   - A Roman province name surviving into Swiss modernity — coins, stamps, the ".ch" domain
   - The Franks came through and left their language; the province name stayed

2. **The typeface**
   - Helvetica, designed in 1957, named for the province
   - Typography's genealogy with empire — how a Roman administrative label became the most ubiquitous font on earth
   - Design history as cultural history

3. **Zurich as collision zone**
   - Germanic, Latinate, and Celtic worlds collapsed into each other here
   - Nobody sorted it out. They made watches and stayed neutral.

### BOOK B (Historical Linguistics): "Züritüütsch and the High German Consonant Shift"

**The sound change that split Germanic in two, frozen in Swiss dialect.**

#### Structural Sketch

1. **The High German consonant shift**
   - The isogloss that separates High German from Low German
   - Swiss German preserves features on both sides — a living fossil of the transition zone

2. **Züritüütsch**
   - Zurich German is not Standard German — it's barely mutually intelligible
   - What it sounds like, what it preserves, why the Swiss don't write it

3. **The author in Zurich**
   - Arriving from the Romance world into the Germanic sound-world
   - Standard German vs. the dialect nobody teaches you
   - The "stumble in the vernacular" moment for Zurich
